# Editorial Copywriting Skill

This skill governs the voice, tone, and prose style of **"What Are We Really Paying For When We Buy a Smartphone?"**.

---

## 1. WHEN TO ACTIVATE

Activate this skill whenever:
- Drafting or editing chapter narratives, introductions, or section conclusions.
- Authoring component telemetry callouts and 3D teardown annotations.
- Writing data visualization captions, tooltips, and explanatory legends.
- Formulating button labels, navigation links, and micro-copy.
- Drafting summary findings and methodology statements.

---

## 2. VOICE & EDITORIAL PRINCIPLES

1. **Authoritative & Dispassionate:** Write like a seasoned investigative technology journalist or industrial analyst. Critique marketing claims using empirical evidence, not emotional rhetoric.
2. **Technical Precision:** Use exact industry nomenclature (e.g., *die size, thermal throttling, UFS 4.0 flash storage, LPDDR5X RAM, OLED sub-pixel layout, CBU customs duty*). Never dumb down concepts; explain them with clarity.
3. **Restrained & Sophisticated:** Let the data deliver the punchline. Avoid breathless sensationalism, exclamation marks, or hyperbolic proclamations.
4. **Natural Cadence:** Vary sentence lengths to create an engaging rhythm. Balance sharp, direct observations with detailed analytical reasoning.

---

## 3. MANDATORY WRITING RULES & PROHIBITED PATTERNS

### The Banned Cliché Catalog
Strictly forbid the following hallmarks of generic AI writing:
- **Banned Words:** *Delve, tapestry, testament to, game-changer, revolutionary, landscape, powerhouse, beacon, unravel, nestled, bespoke, vibrant, paramount.*
- **Banned Sentence Starters:** *"In conclusion...", "It's important to remember...", "At the end of the day...", "Moreover, it is worth noting that..."*
- **Banned Typographic Crutches:**
  - Zero decorative emojis in editorial prose or headings.
  - Zero gratuitous em-dashes (`—`) used as filler transitions; use clean commas or separate sentences.
  - Zero informal tildes (`~`) in prose; write "approximately" or use exact numbers.
  - Zero exclamation marks outside quoted historical slogans.

### Style Standards
- **Language:** Standard American English spelling and grammar (*modeling, color, analyze*).
- **Numbers:** Write numbers $\ge 10$ as numerals (e.g., *12 models, 120Hz, $450*). Use words for numbers under 10 when not expressing a physical unit or specification (*three distinct tiers, but 8GB RAM*).
- **Currencies:** Always use proper symbols and conventions (`$385`, `₹34,999`, `Rp 6.006.000`).

---

## 5. QUALITY CHECKLIST

Before publishing any text snippet, verify:
- [ ] Is the prose completely free of banned words (*delve, tapestry, game-changer, etc.*)?
- [ ] Are emojis completely absent from headings and body text?
- [ ] Are claims grounded in concrete data rather than vague generalizations?
- [ ] Is the tone investigative, objective, and intellectually honest?
- [ ] Are technical specifications named with exact, accurate terminology?
- [ ] Does the reading rhythm feel natural, articulate, and human?

---

## 6. PROJECT CONTEXT & EXPLICIT LIMITATIONS

- **Context:** The editorial voice bridges technical engineering reality and consumer economics.
- **Limitations:** Copywriters must never invent quotes, manufacturer justifications, or statistical findings. All prose must defer to verified data assets.
